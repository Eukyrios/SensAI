# Basic sentence order [N5]
Japanese is subject–object–verb: the verb comes last. 私は毎日日本語を勉強します — I study Japanese every day. Particles mark each part's role, so the parts before the verb can move, but the verb stays at the end.

# Noun sentences with です [N5]
X は Y です means "X is Y": これは本です — this is a book. Negative: じゃないです or ではありません. Past: でした. Past negative: じゃなかったです or ではありませんでした. です is the polite form of だ.

# Questions with か [N5]
Add か at the end of a polite sentence to make a question: 学生ですか — are you a student? No question mark is needed in formal writing. Question words: 何 (what), 誰 (who), どこ (where), いつ (when), どう (how).

# Existence: いる and ある [N5]
いる is used for living things (people, animals), ある for objects and plants. The place takes に and the thing that exists takes が: 公園に犬がいます — there is a dog in the park; 机の上に本があります — there is a book on the desk.

# Counting objects: counters [N5]
Japanese counts with counters after the number. つ is the general counter for things up to ten (ひとつ, ふたつ, みっつ). 本 (hon) counts long thin objects, 枚 (mai) flat objects like paper and shirts, 匹 (hiki) small animals, 人 (nin) people (with ひとり and ふたり for one and two). The counter usually follows the object and its particle: りんごを三つ買いました.

# Telling the time and days [N5]
時 (ji) is the hour, 分 (fun/pun) the minute: 三時十五分 (sanji juugofun). 半 means half past: 七時半. Days of the week end in 曜日: 月曜日 (Monday), 火曜日, 水曜日, 木曜日, 金曜日, 土曜日, 日曜日 (Sunday). A specific time takes に: 月曜日に会いましょう.

# Giving reasons: から and ので [N4]
Clause + から gives a reason, subjective and direct: 寒いから、窓を閉めます. Clause + ので is softer and more polite: 雨なので、行きません — since it is raining, I won't go. With nouns and な-adjectives, ので needs な: 学生なので.
