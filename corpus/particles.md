# Particle は (wa): the topic [N5]
は marks the topic of the sentence: what the sentence is about. It is written は but pronounced "wa". 私は学生です (watashi wa gakusei desu) — I am a student. The topic is often already known to the listener, and the rest of the sentence comments on it.

# Particle が (ga): the subject [N5]
が marks the grammatical subject, often new information or the thing being identified. 猫がいます (neko ga imasu) — there is a cat. Question words such as 誰 and 何 take が, never は, when they are the subject: 誰が来ましたか (dare ga kimashita ka) — who came?

# は vs が: choosing between them [N5]
Use は to set a topic and comment on it; use が to point at the subject, to introduce something new, or to answer "who/what". 私は田中です answers "who are you?" about me as the topic; 私が田中です means "I am the Tanaka (you are looking for)". Likes, dislikes, skills and needs take が for their object: 猫が好きです (neko ga suki desu) — I like cats. In a sentence with both, は often marks the topic and が the subject inside the comment: 象は鼻が長い — elephants have long trunks.

# Particle を (o): the direct object [N5]
を marks the direct object of an action verb. It is pronounced "o". パンを食べます (pan o tabemasu) — I eat bread. Motion verbs such as 行く, 来る and 帰る do not take a destination with を: the destination takes に or へ. を is used with motion verbs only for the place moved through or left: 公園を歩く (walk through the park), 家を出る (leave the house).

# Particle に (ni): destination, time and existence [N5]
に marks the destination of movement: 学校に行きます (gakkou ni ikimasu) — I go to school. It marks a specific point in time: 七時に起きます — I get up at seven; relative time words like 今日, 明日 and 毎日 take no に. It marks where something or someone exists with いる and ある: 部屋に猫がいます. It marks the indirect object or the person met: 友達に会います — I meet a friend; 先生に聞きます — I ask the teacher.

# Particle へ (e): direction [N5]
へ marks the direction of movement and is pronounced "e". 日本へ行きます (nihon e ikimasu) — I go to Japan. With motion verbs へ and に are usually interchangeable; へ stresses the direction, に the arrival point. Only に is used for time or existence.

# Particle で (de): place of action and means [N5]
で marks the place where an action happens: 図書館で勉強します (toshokan de benkyou shimasu) — I study at the library. Compare に for where something exists: 図書館にいます — I am in the library. で also marks the means or tool: 電車で行きます — I go by train; 日本語で話します — I speak in Japanese.

# Particles と and も [N5]
と joins nouns in a complete list ("and") and marks a companion ("with"): 友達と映画を見ました — I watched a movie with a friend. も means "also, too" and replaces は, が and を: 私も学生です — I am a student too. With other particles it follows them: 東京にも行きました.

# Particle の: possession and linking [N5]
の links two nouns; the first describes the second. 私の本 (watashi no hon) — my book; 日本語の先生 — a teacher of Japanese. It is not used between an adjective and a noun: 大きい本, not 大きいの本.

# Particles から and まで [N5]
から means "from" a starting point in space or time, まで means "until, as far as". 九時から五時まで働きます — I work from nine to five. After a clause, から also means "because": 雨だから行きません — because it is raining, I won't go.
