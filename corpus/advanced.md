# Passive form [N3]
The passive says something is done to the subject. Godan: final u-sound → a-sound + れる (読まれる); ichidan: drop る + られる (食べられる); する → される. The doer takes に: 先生に褒められました — I was praised by the teacher. The suffering passive expresses being negatively affected: 雨に降られた — I got caught in the rain.

# Respectful language: sonkeigo [N3]
Sonkeigo raises the person you talk about. Special verbs: いらっしゃる (to be, go, come), 召し上がる (to eat, drink), おっしゃる (to say). Regular pattern: お + verb stem + になる (お帰りになる). Use it for customers, superiors and guests, never for yourself.

# Humble language: kenjougo [N3]
Kenjougo lowers yourself to respect the listener. Special verbs: 参る (to go, come), いただく (to eat, receive), 申す (to say), おる (to be). Regular pattern: お + verb stem + する (お持ちします — let me carry it). Use it for your own actions toward a superior or customer.

# ざるを得ない: cannot help but [N2]
Verb ない-stem + ざるを得ない means "have no choice but to": 行かざるを得ない — I have no choice but to go. する becomes せざるを得ない. It is formal and written; in conversation, しかない or なければならない are more common.

# にもかかわらず: despite [N2]
Noun or plain clause + にもかかわらず means "despite, in spite of": 雨にもかかわらず、試合は行われた — despite the rain, the match took place. It is formal and mostly written.
